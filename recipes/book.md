## Freeman House Chai Concentrate (Copycat)

Ginger forward, demerara sweetened, restrained spice blend (no vanilla, star anise, fennel, or nutmeg).

**Tags:** chai, tea, copycat, make-ahead, batch
**Time:** 45 min

**Yield:** about 4 cups concentrate (8 servings)

**Ingredients**
- 4 cups filtered water
- 2 inch piece fresh ginger (about 30g), sliced and smashed
- 10 to 12 green cardamom pods, cracked open
- 2 cinnamon sticks, broken into pieces
- 8 whole cloves
- 1 tsp whole black peppercorns, lightly crushed
- 4 to 5 tbsp loose Assam CTC tea (or 5 Assam tea bags)
- 1/4 cup demerara sugar (adjust to taste)

**Method**
1. **Prep spices:** Crack cardamom pods, lightly crush peppercorns, slice ginger into 1/8 inch coins and smash, break cinnamon into pieces.
2. **Toast:** Dry toast cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, and peppercorns over medium heat 1 to 2 minutes until fragrant. Don't skip.
3. **Simmer:** Add water and ginger. Bring to a boil, then cover and gently simmer 25 to 30 minutes.
4. **Sweeten:** Stir in demerara sugar while hot until dissolved.
5. **Steep tea:** Off heat, add tea. Cover and steep exactly 5 minutes. Don't squeeze bags.
6. **Strain and store:** Strain, cool to room temp, bottle, refrigerate.

**Serving:** 1 part concentrate to 1 part milk (oat milk works great). Heat until steaming (don't boil) or pour over ice.

**Notes**
- Assam CTC tea gives the malty backbone. Fresh ginger is non-negotiable. Turbinado can sub for demerara.
- Keeps 2 weeks refrigerated (best in 7 to 10 days). Freeze in ice cube trays up to 3 months.
- Bitter? Tea over-steeped or boiled. Flat? Increase ginger 50%.
- Dirty chai: add a shot of espresso. Stronger spice: simmer 40 minutes before adding tea. Less sweet: 2 to 3 tbsp sugar.

## Thai Milk Tea (Cha Yen)

**Tags:** tea, thai, iced, quick
**Time:** 10 min

**Ingredients**
- 2 tbsp Thai tea mix (ChaTraMue brand)
- 1 cup boiling water
- 2 tbsp sweetened condensed milk
- 2 to 3 tbsp evaporated milk (or half-and-half or coconut cream)
- Ice

**Method**
1. Brew tea mix in boiling water 5 minutes. Strain through fine mesh or cloth.
2. Stir in condensed milk while hot.
3. Pour over a tall glass of ice.
4. Top with evaporated milk. Don't stir, let it cascade.

**Street style move:** pour back and forth between cups to aerate before serving.

## Reed's Style Extra Strong Ginger Beer

**Tags:** ginger, fermented, non-alcoholic, project
**Time:** about 1 week

**Ingredients**
- 12 to 16 oz fresh ginger (3 to 4x a normal recipe)
- 1.5 cups sugar
- Juice of 3 to 4 lemons or limes
- 1/4 cup honey
- 1/4 tsp cayenne (optional)
- 1/4 cup ginger bug liquid OR 1/4 tsp champagne yeast
- 8 cups filtered water
- Optional: 1/4 cup pineapple juice

**Method**
1. Grate or food process ginger (skin on). Boil in 4 cups water 15 to 20 minutes.
2. Off heat, add sugar, honey, and cayenne. Steep 1 to 2 hours (or overnight). Strain, pressing the pulp hard.
3. Add citrus juice and remaining 4 cups cold water. Cool to room temp.
4. Add ginger bug or champagne yeast.
5. Bottle in pressure-rated flip-top bottles, 1.5 inches headspace. Ferment 3 to 4 days at room temp, burping twice daily.
6. Refrigerate once very fizzy. Let it sit 3 to 5 days in the fridge before drinking.

**Safety:** This much sugar creates serious pressure. Burp bottles and refrigerate promptly.

## Traditional Ginger Beer (Ginger Bug Method)

**Tags:** ginger, fermented, ginger bug, non-alcoholic, project
**Time:** about 2 weeks

**Ginger bug (5 to 7 days)**
- 2 to 3 inches fresh ginger, unpeeled, grated
- 2 tbsp sugar
- 2 tbsp dechlorinated water

Combine in a jar, cover with cloth. Each day add 1 tbsp grated ginger and 1 tbsp sugar, stir. Ready when bubbly and yeasty smelling. Feed weekly at room temp or every 2 weeks refrigerated.

**Ginger beer**
- 6 to 8 oz fresh ginger, grated
- 1 to 1.5 cups sugar
- Juice of 2 to 3 lemons
- 1/4 cup strained ginger bug liquid
- 8 cups filtered water

**Method**
1. Boil ginger in 4 cups water 10 to 15 minutes. Add sugar, steep 30 to 60 minutes.
2. Strain. Add lemon juice and remaining 4 cups cold water. Cool to room temp.
3. Stir in ginger bug liquid. Bottle with 2 inches headspace.
4. Ferment 2 to 4 days at room temp, burping daily. Refrigerate once fizzy. Drink within 2 to 3 weeks.

## Fluffy Pancakes

**Tags:** pancakes, weekend, vegetarian, quick
**Time:** 30 min

**Serves 4 (about 8 pancakes)**

**Ingredients**
- 1.5 cups all-purpose flour
- 2 tbsp granulated sugar
- 2 tsp baking powder
- 1/2 tsp salt
- 1.25 cups milk
- 1 large egg
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter, melted
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 1 tbsp butter or oil for the pan

**Method**
1. Whisk dry ingredients in one bowl, wet ingredients in another.
2. Pour wet into dry and stir just until no dry streaks remain. Lumps are fine.
3. Rest batter 5 minutes while the pan heats.
4. Heat nonstick skillet over medium-low, add butter.
5. Pour 1/4 cup batter per pancake. Cook until bubbles form and edges set (about 2.5 minutes).
6. Flip once, cook about 1.5 minutes more. Hold in a 200°F oven on a rack.

**Notes**
- Buttermilk swap: 1.25 cups buttermilk, reduce baking powder to 1 tsp and add 1/2 tsp baking soda.
- Don't press pancakes after flipping. Medium-low heat is the move.

## Buttermilk Cornbread Waffles (Johnny Cakes Inspired)

A riff on Jeni's discontinued Johnny Cakes flavor, with toasted oat crumble and lemony blueberry preserves.

**Tags:** waffles, brunch, weekend, vegetarian
**Time:** 45 min

**Serves 4**

**Waffles**
- 1 cup fine yellow cornmeal
- 1 cup all-purpose flour
- 3 tbsp light brown sugar
- 1.5 tsp baking powder
- 1/2 tsp baking soda
- 3/4 tsp fine salt
- 1.75 cups buttermilk
- 2 large eggs
- 6 tbsp unsalted butter, melted
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

**Oat crumble**
- 1/2 cup old-fashioned rolled oats
- 1 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 tbsp light brown sugar
- Pinch of flaky salt

**Topping**
- 1/2 cup blueberry preserves
- 1 tsp fresh lemon juice
- Pinch of fine salt
- 1/4 cup fresh blueberries (optional)
- 1/2 cup pure maple syrup

**Method**
1. Preheat waffle iron to medium-high. Oven to 200°F for holding.
2. Whisk dry ingredients. Separately whisk buttermilk, eggs, butter, vanilla.
3. Combine just until no flour streaks. Rest 10 minutes.
4. Crumble: melt butter in a skillet, add oats, sugar, salt. Toast 3 to 4 minutes until deep golden. Cool on a plate.
5. Warm preserves until loose, stir in lemon juice and salt. Don't cook it down.
6. Cook waffles (1/2 to 3/4 cup batter each) 4 to 5 minutes until deep golden. Hold on oven rack.
7. Plate: preserves, fresh blueberries, crumble, maple syrup. Pat of butter optional.

**Notes:** Use fine cornmeal, not coarse polenta. Make crumble same day. Optional scoop of vanilla ice cream or crème fraîche.

## Golden Diner Pancakes

Sam Yoo's tall, yeast-risen buttermilk pancakes from Golden Diner in Manhattan's Chinatown. They're cooked one per skillet for height, then drenched in maple-honey syrup and topped with berry compote and salted maple-honey butter.

**Tags:** pancakes, brunch, weekend, project, nyt cooking
**Time:** 1 hr 45 min

**Makes 4 to 6 large pancakes (3 to 6 servings)**

**Pancake batter**
- 2 1/4 tsp (7 grams) active dry yeast (one 1/4-ounce packet)
- 2 cups (260 grams) all-purpose flour, divided
- 1 1/4 cups (300 grams) buttermilk
- 2 tbsp sugar
- 3/4 tsp baking soda
- 1/2 tsp fine salt
- 2 large eggs
- 1/4 cup (60 grams) canola or other neutral oil

**Maple-honey butter**
- 1/2 cup (113 grams) unsalted butter, softened
- 3 tbsp honey
- 2 tbsp pure maple syrup
- 3/4 tsp fine salt

**Maple-honey syrup**
- 1/2 cup (113 grams) unsalted butter
- 1/3 cup (100 grams) honey
- 1 1/2 tbsp maple syrup
- 1 tsp soy sauce
- 1/2 tsp fine salt

**Berry compote**
- 14 oz (400 grams) mixed berries, such as blueberries, raspberries and stemmed strawberries cut to the size of the small berries
- 1/3 cup (67 grams) sugar
- 1 1/2 tsp cornstarch
- 1 lemon

**Method**
1. **Pre-ferment:** Whisk the yeast with 1 cup of the flour in a small bowl. Warm the buttermilk with 1/4 cup (60 grams) water over medium-low, stirring often, until lukewarm (about 100°F), about 5 minutes. Whisk it into the flour until smooth, cover and let sit 1 hour.
2. **Maple-honey butter:** Whisk the softened butter, honey, maple syrup and salt until smooth. Keep at room temperature if using within a few hours, or refrigerate airtight up to 1 week (soften and rewhisk before serving).
3. **Maple-honey syrup:** In a small saucepan over medium-low, heat the butter, honey, maple syrup, soy sauce and salt, whisking, until the butter melts. Whisk in 1 1/2 tbsp water until emulsified, then keep warm on the lowest heat.
4. **Berry compote:** Toss the berries with the sugar and cornstarch. Heat a large, deep skillet over high until a drop of water sizzles away instantly. Add the berries and cook, stirring now and then, until the blueberries look ready to pop, about 2 minutes. Take off the heat.
5. **Oven:** Heat the oven to 350°F with a rack in the center, and set a wire rack inside a sheet pan on it.
6. **Batter:** Whisk the remaining 1 cup flour with the sugar, baking soda and salt. In a large bowl, whisk the eggs and oil, scrape in the pre-ferment, add the dry mix, and stir gently just until no dry spots remain. Lumps are fine.
7. **Cook:** Heat one or two 7- to 8-inch nonstick skillets over medium until very hot; don't grease them. Fill with batter about 1/3 inch deep, smoothing it to the edges. Cook until the bottom is crisp and golden, 2 to 4 minutes, lowering the heat if it browns too fast. Flip and cook 1 to 3 minutes more, tucking in the edges to dome the pancake.
8. **Finish in the oven:** Move each pancake to the rack in the oven until the center is cooked through, 2 to 5 minutes (check with a paring knife). Repeat with the remaining batter, reheating the pan between pancakes.
9. **Serve:** Set one or two hot pancakes on each plate and drench with the warm syrup right away. Spoon on the berry compote, add a scoop of maple-honey butter and zest the lemon over everything.

**Notes**
- Recipe from Sam Yoo, chef and an owner of Golden Diner, adapted by Genevieve Ko for NYT Cooking.
- Nonstick pans work best because they need no grease. Extremely well-seasoned cast iron also works.
- At the restaurant, the butter is shaped into a football-shaped quenelle with two spoons.

*Source: NYT Cooking (Sam Yoo, adapted by Genevieve Ko): https://cooking.nytimes.com/search?q=Golden%20Diner%20Pancakes*

## Sugar Moon Espresso Cornflake Cookies (Copycat)

Sugar Moon's espresso salted caramel cornflake cookie: a chewy brown sugar espresso cookie piled with salted caramel cornflakes. The topping is made like caramel corn, with cornflakes swapped in for extreme crunch. The cornflakes go on top only; they aren't mixed into the dough.

**Image:** images/sugar-moon-espresso-cornflake-cookie.jpg
**Tags:** cookies, copycat, espresso, salted caramel, make-ahead
**Time:** 1 hr 45 min (plus chilling)

**Makes about 16 large cookies**

**Salted caramel cornflakes**
- 3 cups cornflakes
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1/2 cup light brown sugar, packed
- 2 tbsp light corn syrup
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1/4 tsp baking soda
- 1/4 tsp vanilla extract

**Chewy brown sugar espresso dough**
- 1 cup (2 sticks) unsalted butter, room temp
- 1 1/4 cups dark brown sugar, packed
- 1/4 cup granulated sugar
- 1 egg plus 1 egg yolk
- 2 tsp vanilla extract
- 2 1/4 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp baking soda
- 3/4 tsp kosher salt
- 2 tbsp finely ground espresso (real grind, not instant)
- 1 tbsp instant espresso powder
- Flaky sea salt (Maldon) to finish

**Method**
1. **Caramel:** Heat the oven to 250°F and line a sheet pan with parchment. In a saucepan, melt butter, brown sugar, corn syrup and salt. Boil without stirring 3 minutes.
2. **Coat:** Off heat, stir in baking soda and vanilla (it will foam up). Pour over the cornflakes in a large bowl and fold gently until every flake is glossy.
3. **Bake the cornflakes:** Spread on the sheet pan and bake 30 minutes, stirring every 10 minutes, until glassy and dry. Cool completely, then break into clusters.
4. **Dough:** Cream butter and both sugars 3 minutes until fluffy. Beat in the egg, yolk and vanilla. Whisk the flour, baking soda, salt, ground espresso and espresso powder, then mix in until just combined.
5. **Rest:** Refrigerate the dough at least 1 hour (overnight is better for chew and flavor).
6. **Bake:** Heat the oven to 350°F. Scoop 2 oz balls 3 inches apart on parchment. Bake 9 minutes until the edges are just set.
7. **Top:** Pull the pan, pile a generous handful of caramel cornflakes on each cookie and press lightly. Return to the oven 2 to 3 minutes so the caramel softens and fuses to the cookie.
8. **Finish:** Sprinkle flaky salt immediately. Cool on the pan 10 minutes; centers should stay soft and chewy.

**Notes**
- Based on Sugar Moon's own description (Aug 14, 2021): "chewy brown sugar espresso cookie… topped with salted caramel cornflakes (caramel corn but substituted corn flakes for extreme crunch)."
- All brown sugar (mostly dark) gives the chewy, crackly, toffee-colored base in the photo.
- Make extra cornflakes. They keep 1 week airtight and are great over ice cream.

## Bakery Cookie Skillet

For reheating a store-bought bakery cookie into a skillet dessert.

## Soft and Chewy Oatmeal Chocolate Chip Cookies

Danielle's oatmeal chocolate chip cookies from Live Well Bake Often: packed with rolled oats and chocolate chips, soft and chewy, and easy to make.

**Image:** images/oatmeal-chocolate-chip-cookies.jpg
**Tags:** cookies, oatmeal, chocolate, make-ahead, freezer-friendly
**Time:** 57 min (includes 30 min chill)

**Makes 24 cookies**

**Ingredients**
- 1 cup (125 grams) all-purpose flour, spooned and leveled
- 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1/2 tsp baking soda
- 1/4 tsp salt
- 1 1/2 cups (150 grams) old-fashioned rolled oats
- 1/2 cup (115 grams) unsalted butter, softened
- 1/2 cup (100 grams) light brown sugar, packed
- 1/4 cup (50 grams) granulated sugar
- 1 large egg, room temperature
- 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
- 1 cup (190 grams) semisweet chocolate chips

**Method**
1. **Dry mix:** In a medium bowl, whisk the flour, cinnamon, baking soda and salt. Stir in the rolled oats and set aside.
2. **Cream:** In a stand mixer with the paddle (or with a hand mixer), beat the butter, brown sugar and granulated sugar 1 to 2 minutes, until well combined.
3. **Egg:** Mix in the egg and vanilla, scraping down the sides of the bowl as needed.
4. **Combine:** Add the dry ingredients and mix on low until just combined, then mix in the chocolate chips on low until evenly distributed.
5. **Chill:** Cover tightly and refrigerate at least 30 minutes.
6. **Heat:** Meanwhile, preheat the oven to 350°F (177°C). Line two large baking sheets with parchment or silicone mats.
7. **Scoop:** Use a 1.5 tbsp cookie scoop to portion the dough onto the sheets, leaving a little room between each. Gently press each ball to flatten slightly.
8. **Bake:** Bake 10 to 13 minutes, until the tops are set.
9. **Cool:** Cool on the baking sheet 5 to 10 minutes, then move to a wire rack to cool completely.

**Notes**
- Store in an airtight container on the counter for up to 1 week.
- Use old-fashioned rolled oats for the chewiest texture. Quick oats work, but the texture won't be quite the same.
- Only have salted butter? Cut the salt to a tiny pinch.
- Make ahead: the dough keeps 2 to 3 days, covered tightly in the fridge. If it's too cold to scoop, let it sit 20 minutes at room temperature.
- Freeze dough: scoop, roll and gently flatten the balls on a parchment-lined sheet, freeze, then store in a freezer bag up to 3 months. Bake from frozen, adding 1 to 2 minutes if needed.
- Baked cookies freeze well up to 3 months. Thaw to room temperature before serving.

## Pan de Muerto

Soft, buttery, orange-scented sweet bread for Día de Muertos (Nov 1–2), topped with crossed dough "bones" and a coat of sugar.

**Tags:** mexican, bread, holiday, day of the dead, project
**Time:** 4 hr (mostly rising)

**Makes 2 medium loaves (8 to 10 servings)**

**Dough**
- 1/2 cup whole milk
- 1 tsp anise seeds (optional)
- 3 1/2 cups (450g) all-purpose or bread flour
- 1/2 cup (100g) sugar
- 2 1/4 tsp instant yeast (1 packet)
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- Zest of 2 oranges
- 3 large eggs plus 2 yolks, room temp
- 1 tbsp orange blossom water (agua de azahar)
- 1/2 cup (113g) unsalted butter, softened and cubed

**Egg wash**
- 1 egg
- 1 tbsp milk

**Topping**
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter, melted
- 1/2 cup sugar, rubbed with a little orange zest

**Method**
1. **Infuse:** Warm the milk with the anise seeds until steaming. Steep 10 minutes, strain, and cool to lukewarm (about 100°F).
2. **Mix:** In a stand mixer, combine flour, sugar, yeast, salt and orange zest. Add the milk, eggs, yolks and orange blossom water. Mix on low 5 minutes until a shaggy dough forms.
3. **Butter:** On medium, add butter a few cubes at a time. Knead 10 to 12 minutes until smooth, glossy and elastic. It will be soft and tacky, so resist adding much flour.
4. **First rise:** Cover and let rise somewhere warm 1.5 to 2 hours until doubled. (Or refrigerate overnight; cold dough is much easier to shape.)
5. **Divide:** Set aside about 1/5 of the dough (around 150g) for decorations. Split the rest in half and shape each into a tight, smooth ball. Place on a parchment-lined sheet and flatten slightly.
6. **Bones:** From the reserved dough, roll 2 small marbles for the skulls. Roll the rest into 4 ropes about as long as the loaves are wide. Press with spread fingers as you roll to make knobby "bones."
7. **Decorate:** Brush the loaves with egg wash. Lay 2 bones across each loaf in an X, then press a skull ball in the center. Brush again to glue everything down.
8. **Second rise:** Cover loosely and let rise 45 to 60 minutes until puffy. Preheat the oven to 350°F.
9. **Bake:** Brush gently with egg wash. Bake 30 to 35 minutes until deep golden and 190°F inside. Tent with foil around 20 minutes if browning fast.
10. **Finish:** Cool 10 minutes. Brush all over with melted butter and sprinkle heavily with orange sugar.

**Notes**
- No orange blossom water? Use 1 tsp vanilla plus extra orange zest.
- Keeps 2 days wrapped at room temp. Refresh 5 minutes in a 300°F oven.
- Serve with Mexican hot chocolate, champurrado, or a latte made with the Freeman House chai.

## Latino Foodie Pan de Muerto

Art Rodriguez's pan de muerto from Latino Foodie: an anise and orange scented sweet bread made as six individual loaves, each crossed with dough bones, brushed with butter and finished with sugar.

**Image:** images/latino-foodie-pan-de-muerto.jpg
**Tags:** mexican, bread, holiday, day of the dead, project
**Time:** 3 hr 45 min (2 hr 45 min proofing)

**Makes 6 loaves**

**Dough**
- 1/2 cup whole milk
- 1/2 cup water
- 2 packages active dry yeast (or 4 1/2 tsp)
- 5 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
- 1/2 cup (1 stick) unsalted butter, room temp
- 1/2 cup granulated sugar
- 4 large eggs
- Zest of 2 medium oranges
- 1 tbsp whole anise seed
- 1 tsp kosher salt

**Topping**
- 1/2 cup (1 stick) unsalted butter
- 1/2 cup sugar

**Method**
1. **Bloom:** In a medium glass bowl, whisk the milk, water and yeast. Let it activate in a warm spot until foamy, 5 to 10 minutes.
2. **Sponge:** Whisk in 1/2 cup of the flour. Rest uncovered until doubled in size, about 25 minutes.
3. **Cream:** In a stand mixer with the paddle, cream the butter and sugar on medium until fluffy, about 2 minutes.
4. **Eggs:** Beat in the eggs one at a time on medium until fully incorporated, about 1 minute.
5. **Add the sponge:** Switch to the dough hook, add the yeast mixture and mix on medium until combined, about 2 minutes.
6. **Flour:** On low, add the remaining flour about 1/2 cup at a time, scraping down the sides, until the dough pulls away from the bowl, about 5 minutes. It may stay a little tacky.
7. **Flavor:** Add the orange zest, anise seed and salt. Mix with the dough hook about 5 minutes more, until less tacky and the dough comes off the sides easily.
8. **First rise:** Knead briefly by hand into a ball. Place in a greased bowl, cover with plastic wrap and let double in a warm place, 1 1/2 to 2 hours.
9. **Divide:** Gently punch down the dough, turn it out onto a clean surface and divide into 6 equal pieces.
10. **Shape:** From one piece, cut off 3 small pieces (about 1 tbsp or 1/2 oz each) for the bones. Roll the large piece into a tight ball and set it on a baking sheet. Roll the small pieces into balls, then roll two of them into long, finger-like bones.
11. **Decorate:** Lightly brush the loaf with water. Lay the two bones over it in an X, poke down where they cross, and set the last small ball on top. Repeat with the remaining dough for 6 loaves.
12. **Second rise:** Cover with plastic wrap and let rise in a warm spot, about 45 minutes. Preheat the oven to 350°F for the last 15 minutes of proofing.
13. **Bake:** Bake until golden brown, 25 to 30 minutes, tenting with foil if they brown too fast. Let cool completely.
14. **Topping:** Melt the butter and let it cool. Brush it over the loaves, then sprinkle generously with sugar.

**Notes**
- Recipe and photo by Art Rodriguez for Latino Foodie.
- Compared with the other Pan de Muerto in this book, this one leans on anise and orange zest instead of orange blossom water, and makes six smaller loaves.

- 1/2 tsp ground nutmeg

## Chocolate Soufflés

Four tall chocolate soufflés: a cocoa, coffee and bittersweet chocolate base lightened with glossy beaten egg whites.

**Tags:** dessert, chocolate, souffle, technique, date night, nyt cooking
**Time:** 45 min

**Serves 4**

**Ingredients**
- Unsalted butter, at room temperature, for the ramekins
- 7 tbsp (87 grams) granulated sugar, plus more for sprinkling and the ramekins
- 3 large egg yolks
- 1/3 cup (80 grams) whole milk
- 2 tbsp brewed coffee
- 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 2 tbsp Dutch-processed cocoa powder, sifted if lumpy
- 4 oz (113 grams) bittersweet chocolate (70 percent cacao), coarsely chopped
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 5 large egg whites, at room temperature
- 3/4 tsp kosher salt

**Method**
1. **Ramekins:** Heat the oven to 425°F with a rack in the center. Brush four 6-ounce ramekins with soft butter, using upward strokes up the sides all the way to the rim. Coat with sugar and tap out the excess.
2. **Base:** Bring about 1 inch of water to a simmer in a medium saucepan. In a heatproof bowl, whisk 3 tbsp (37 grams) of the sugar with 2 of the yolks until pale, light and thick, about 1 minute. Slowly whisk in the milk, then the coffee, then the flour and cocoa until smooth.
3. **Cook the base:** Set the bowl over the simmering water without touching it and whisk constantly until it's like thin pancake batter, faintly holds whisk marks and the foam has subsided, about 5 minutes. Remove from the heat.
4. **Chocolate:** Whisk in the chopped chocolate, let it sit a few minutes to melt, then whisk smooth. Whisk in the remaining yolk and the vanilla. Let cool slightly, but not completely or the chocolate will set.
5. **Whites:** In a clean metal or glass bowl (not plastic), beat the whites and salt on medium-low until frothy, about 20 seconds, then on medium-high until foamy and opaque, about 30 seconds. Stream in the remaining 4 tbsp (50 grams) sugar and beat just until dense, glossy and holding stiff peaks. Don't overbeat.
6. **Fold:** Quickly whisk a third of the whites into the chocolate base, then fold in the rest in 2 additions with a large spatula until almost streak-free.
7. **Fill:** Divide all the batter among the ramekins, filling them to the very top. Tap gently, then level each flush with the rim using an offset spatula or butter knife. Discard any small amount left over.
8. **Prep the tops:** Sprinkle the tops with a thin, even layer of sugar. Run your thumb around the inside rim of each ramekin to clear a ring of batter so they rise straight up. Wipe the rims and outsides clean.
9. **Bake:** Set the ramekins on a sheet pan, put them in the oven and immediately lower the heat to 375°F. Bake until risen, firm and springy on top with a slight wobble, 15 to 20 minutes. Serve immediately.

**Notes**
- By Claire Saffitz for NYT Cooking.
- The keys: stable, glossy whites; working quickly; and thoroughly buttered ramekins so the batter can climb.

*Source: NYT Cooking (Claire Saffitz): https://cooking.nytimes.com/search?q=Chocolate%20Souffl%C3%A9s*

## Conchas

Soft, brioche-style Mexican sweet buns with a crumbly sugar topping scored like a seashell. Choose vanilla, café con leche or cocoa.

**Tags:** mexican, bread, pan dulce, breakfast, project, nyt cooking
**Time:** 1 1/2 hr (plus 4 to 24 hr chilled rise and 1 1/2 to 2 hr proof)

**Makes 15 buns**

**Dough**
- 4 cups (520 grams) all-purpose flour (or bread flour), plus more for dusting
- 7 tbsp (87 grams) granulated sugar
- 2 tsp coarse kosher salt (Morton)
- 1 tbsp instant yeast
- 3/4 cup (175 grams) whole milk, at room temperature
- 4 large eggs, at room temperature
- 2 tsp vanilla extract
- 13 tbsp (185 grams) unsalted butter, diced and at room temperature, plus more for greasing

**Vanilla topping** (pick one topping; each covers all 15 buns)
- 1 1/2 cups (180 grams) confectioners' sugar
- 1 1/2 cups (184 grams) all-purpose flour, plus more as needed
- 1 cup (180 grams) vegetable shortening
- 1 tbsp vanilla extract

**Café con leche topping** (or this one)
- 1 1/2 cups (180 grams) confectioners' sugar
- 1 1/2 cups (184 grams) all-purpose flour, plus more as needed
- 1 cup (180 grams) vegetable shortening
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 2 tbsp instant espresso mixed with 2 tsp boiling water

**Cocoa topping** (or this one)
- 1 1/2 cups (180 grams) confectioners' sugar
- 1 1/3 cups (170 grams) all-purpose flour, plus more as needed
- 1 cup (180 grams) vegetable shortening
- 1/4 cup (22 grams) cocoa powder mixed with 3 tbsp boiling water

**Method**
1. **Mix the dough:** In a stand mixer with the dough hook, whisk the flour, sugar, salt and yeast. Add the milk, eggs and vanilla and mix on the lowest speed about 20 seconds. Raise to medium-high and knead until the dough slaps the bowl and forms a smooth, elastic ball around the hook, 10 to 15 minutes. Stay close; the mixer will walk.
2. **Add the butter:** On low, add the butter in 3 or 4 additions, then knead on medium-high until the dough is very soft, silky and shiny and hugs the hook again, 8 to 12 minutes.
3. **Cold rise:** Move the dough to a buttered bowl. Stretch and fold: lift the dough and fold it over itself, rotate the bowl 90 degrees and repeat 3 more times. Cover and refrigerate 4 to 24 hours.
4. **Shape:** Line three 13-by-18-inch sheet pans with parchment. On a lightly floured counter, cut the dough into 15 pieces (about 76 grams each). Pat each flat, gather the edges into the center and pinch, then flip and roll under a cupped hand into a neat ball. Place 5 on each sheet.
5. **Topping:** Beat your chosen topping with the paddle on the lowest speed 20 to 30 seconds, then on medium until smooth and glossy, about 2 minutes.
6. **Top the buns:** Divide the topping into 15 portions (about 30 grams each). With wet hands, roll each into a ball and pat into a 3 1/2-inch round. Lay it over a bun and press lightly around the edge, stopping at least 1/4 inch above the parchment.
7. **Score:** Dip a concha mold or dinner knife in flour and press a shell pattern into the topping without cutting through to the dough.
8. **Proof:** Cover with kitchen towels and let rise in a warm, draft-free spot until noticeably puffed, 1 1/2 to 2 hours. They don't need to double.
9. **Bake:** Heat the oven to 375°F. Bake until golden and puffed, 20 to 22 minutes, baking the third sheet after the first two if needed.
10. **Rest:** Let rest a few minutes before serving.

**Notes**
- By Pati Jinich for NYT Cooking.
- Bread flour gives taller, chewier and slightly less sweet buns.
- To try several flavors, make a half or third of each topping and mix it by hand or with a hand mixer.
- Best the day they're baked. Keep airtight up to 4 days.

*Source: NYT Cooking (Pati Jinich): https://cooking.nytimes.com/search?q=Conchas*

## Jamaican Brown Stew Oxtail (5 lb Practice Batch)

From the Oxtail Off competition prep. This is the home scale practice version. The full 28 lb competition recipe, shopping list, and day-of timeline live in the source chat.

**Tags:** jamaican, oxtail, braise, competition, project, make-ahead
**Time:** 2 days (mostly marinating)

**Marinade**
- 1/3 cup soy sauce
- 2 tbsp browning
- 3 tbsp dark brown sugar
- 1/2 bunch scallion whites, smashed
- 1/2 head garlic, crushed
- 1 inch ginger, grated
- 1/2 bunch thyme (stripped leaves and stems)
- 2 tsp peppercorns, crushed
- 1 tbsp allspice berries, crushed
- 3 tbsp Pickapeppa
- 1 scotch bonnet, pierced
- 1.5 tbsp kosher salt

**Braise and finish**
- 1/4 cup light brown sugar (for blackened sugar)
- Onion, garlic, ginger, scallions (split into two halves)
- 1 whole scotch bonnet, thyme, allspice berries, bay leaves
- 3 cups beef stock, plus water
- 2 tbsp white sugar, 1 tbsp browning (reserved)
- Butter beans

**Spinners**
- 1 cup AP flour, 1 tsp salt, about 1/3 cup water

**Method**
1. **Prep:** Rinse oxtail in cold water with a splash of vinegar or lime. Pat completely dry.
2. **Marinate:** Combine marinade, add oxtail, massage 5 minutes. Refrigerate 24 hours (48 preferred).
3. **Dry:** Pull oxtail, reserve marinade. Pat each piece dry.
4. **Blackened sugar:** Heat Dutch oven over high, add 1/4 cup brown sugar. Stir until nearly black and smoking (about 5 minutes). Carefully add 2 tbsp boiling water (it splatters). Stir into a dark syrup.
5. **Brown:** Add oxtail in 2 batches, coat in caramel, brown about 10 minutes per batch. Remove. Pour off all but 2 tbsp fat.
6. **First aromatic add:** Half the onion, garlic, ginger, scallions, plus whole scotch bonnet, half the thyme, allspice, bay leaves. Stir 5 minutes.
7. **Braise 1:** Return oxtail, juices, and reserved marinade. Add stock and water to almost submerge. Bare simmer, covered, about 1 hour (300°F oven or low stovetop).
8. **Second aromatic add:** Remaining aromatics, 2 tbsp white sugar, 1 tbsp browning.
9. **Braise 2:** About 1 more hour until meat is loose on the bone. Test at 45 minutes.
10. **Spinners (during braise 2):** Mix flour, salt, water into a firm dough. Knead 2 minutes, rest 30 minutes. Roll about 12 tapered 2 inch cylinders.
11. **Pull and strain:** Cool oxtail 15 minutes. Strain sauce through a fine mesh strainer, skim fat hard.
12. **Debone:** Pull meat in rustic chunks. Don't shred.
13. **Reduce:** Reduce sauce on medium-high until visibly thicker (about 15 minutes).
14. **Spinners in:** Simmer 18 to 22 minutes until cooked through with no chalky center.
15. **Finish:** Add butter beans and meat back to the sauce. Thicken with a flour slurry (flour whisked into hot braising liquid) about 15 minutes before serving.

**Key moves:** long marinade, blackened sugar, two-stage aromatics, strained glossy sauce, Pickapeppa and browning over Worcestershire.

*Source: https://claude.ai/chat/b60c9674-a039-4917-a05c-b9f9b5cb5439*

### Competition Plating Options

Presentation ideas for the same braise, from the competition plating chat.

- **Pressed terrine (recommended):** Pack warm deboned meat into molds while still saturated with braising liquid. The gelatin sets it. Slice thick medallions, sear for bark, glaze with the reduction.
- **Short rib style:** Pull meat in the largest chunks possible. Stack 3 to 4 pieces vertically in a ring mold, glazing between layers. Height wins on competition plates.
- **Dumpling:** Wrap deboned meat in wonton wrappers or dumpling dough. Pan fry for a crispy bottom, steam the top. Serve in a shallow pool of reduced braising liquid.
- **Croquette:** Shred meat, bind with reduced braising liquid, form cylinders or balls, bread in panko or fine cornmeal, deep fry. Serve on scotch bonnet aioli or jus.

**Finishing touches**
- Scotch bonnet oil: blend scotch bonnet with neutral oil, then strain. Color and heat without chunks.
- Fresh thyme sprig across the top, thinly sliced scallion greens, a few whole pimento berries on the plate for aroma.

**Scaling note:** For big batches, braise in two pots rather than one giant one. Even consistency matters more than time saved.

## Oxtail and Butter Beans

Jamaican-style oxtail braised until it falls off the bone in a rich, full-bodied gravy, finished with butter beans.

**Tags:** jamaican, oxtail, stew, braise, nyt cooking
**Time:** about 3 1/2 hr

**Serves 4**

**Ingredients**
- 4 pounds oxtails
- 2 tsp coarse kosher salt
- 2 tsp black pepper
- 1/4 cup vegetable oil
- 4 scallions, trimmed and chopped
- 2 large yellow onions, chopped
- 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 2 tbsp Jamaican curry powder
- 2 tsp smoked paprika
- 12 whole pimento seeds (allspice berries)
- 4 fresh thyme sprigs
- 2 Scotch bonnet peppers, pierced with a paring knife
- 2 tbsp chicken stock concentrate or 1 chicken bouillon cube
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 4 tsp browning sauce, preferably Grace
- 4 large garlic cloves, grated
- 1 tsp freshly grated ginger
- 2 (15-ounce) cans butter beans, drained and rinsed

**Method**
1. **Season:** Season the oxtails with the salt and pepper.
2. **Brown:** Heat the oil in a large pot over medium. Brown the oxtails in batches without crowding, scraping up the browned bits, about 3 minutes per side, until deeply crusted. Pour off all but about 2 tbsp oil and set the oxtails aside.
3. **Aromatics:** Add the scallions, onions, Worcestershire, curry powder, paprika, pimento and thyme and sauté until the onions are translucent, about 5 minutes. Add the Scotch bonnets, bouillon, tomato paste and browning and stir 1 minute. Add the garlic and ginger and cook 30 seconds.
4. **Braise:** Return the oxtails with enough liquid to cover them completely. Bring to a boil, then simmer, stirring occasionally, until tender, 2 to 3 hours.
5. **Beans:** Add the butter beans and heat through, about 5 minutes, or 10 to 15 for softer beans.
6. **Serve:** Remove the thyme, Scotch bonnets and any pimento seeds you see. Adjust the seasoning and serve hot with rice and peas and sweet plantains.

**Notes**
- By Millie Peartree for NYT Cooking.
- A simpler one-pot cousin of the competition Jamaican Brown Stew Oxtail in this book.
- The gravy is great sopped up with roti or naan, or over coconut rice and peas.

## Jerk Spiced Duck Tacos (Bon Appétit)

Build-your-own tacos. Low and slow roast renders the fat and makes the meat fall-apart tender.

**Tags:** jamaican, tacos, duck, entertaining, holiday, project
**Time:** 5 hr roast (plus 12 hr marinade)

**Serves 8**

**Ingredients**
- 2 ducks (5 to 5.5 lb each)
- Kosher salt
- 16 habanero chiles, stems removed (keep the seeds; the duck fat tames the heat)
- 2 pieces (2 inch) ginger, scrubbed and crushed
- 1 cup soy sauce
- 1/2 cup unseasoned rice vinegar
- 2 tbsp allspice berries
- 2 whole nutmegs
- 1/2 cup spiced rum
- 4 tsp sugar

**For serving:** hoisin sauce, small flour tortillas, sliced napa cabbage, sliced scallions

**Method**
1. **Score:** With a clean razor blade or very sharp thin knife, cut through the skin and fat but not into the flesh. Salt generously. Place each duck in a large resealable bag.

5. **Serve** taco style with the fixings.

**Notes**
- Keep the ducks whole (don't butterfly) so they self-baste.
- The roast yields 2+ cups of habanero spiced duck fat. Save it for plantains or potatoes.

- Night before: score and start the 12 hour marinade.

- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 1/4 cup breadcrumbs (optional, for structure)

**Method**
1. Soak torn bread in milk 5 minutes. Squeeze out excess, keep damp.
2. Beat egg, add bread, Parm, parsley, garlic, salt, pepper. Add turkey, mix by hand until just combined.

## Creamy Lemon Pesto Pasta

Kirkland pesto turned silky and clingy (not oily) with pasta water, mascarpone, butter, and lemon.

**Tags:** italian, pasta, pesto, lemon, weeknight, vegetarian
**Time:** 20 min

**Serves 2**

**Ingredients**
- 8 oz short pasta (fusilli, gemelli, or rigatoni)
- 1 tbsp kosher salt, for pasta water
- 1/2 cup Kirkland basil pesto
- 2 tbsp mascarpone or ricotta (or heavy cream)
- 1 lemon
- 1 tbsp cold unsalted butter
- 1/3 cup grated Parmesan, plus more to serve
- 1/4 tsp black pepper or red pepper flakes

**Method**
1. **Boil:** Salt a large pot of boiling water and cook the pasta until about 1 minute shy of al dente.
2. **Sauce base:** While it cooks, stir the pesto and mascarpone together in a large bowl. Zest about half the lemon into the bowl.
3. **Save water and drain:** Scoop out about 1 cup of pasta water, then drain. Don't rinse.
4. **Emulsify:** Whisk 2 to 3 tbsp hot pasta water into the pesto mixture until smooth, creamy, and pale green.
5. **Toss:** Add the hot pasta and cold butter. Toss hard for about 30 seconds, adding pasta water a spoonful at a time until glossy and every piece is coated.
6. **Cheese:** Add the Parmesan and toss again until the sauce thickens and clings.
7. **Lemon:** Squeeze in 1 to 2 tsp lemon juice, toss, and taste. Adjust juice and salt.
8. **Serve:** Top with extra zest, Parmesan, and pepper. Serve right away.

**Notes**
- Keep the pesto off direct heat the whole time. Heat breaks the emulsion (oily) and dulls the basil.
- Pasta water is the key: its starch emulsifies the oil, thickens the sauce, and helps it cling. Use a bit less water in the pot for starchier water.
- Juice goes in last so it tastes fresh and doesn't break the cream. Zest carries most of the lemon flavor.
- Leftovers tighten in the fridge; loosen with a splash of water or milk when reheating.

## Roasted Dumpling Squash with Sage Brown Butter

Sweet dumpling squash has thin, edible skin, so there's no peeling. High-heat roasting caramelizes the edges, then a maple sage brown butter goes on top.

**Tags:** squash, vegetarian, fall, thanksgiving, side, weeknight
**Time:** 40 min

**Serves 4**

**Squash**
- 2 sweet dumpling squash (about 1 lb each)
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 3/4 tsp kosher salt
- 1/4 tsp black pepper
- 1/4 tsp ground cinnamon
- Pinch of cayenne or chipotle powder

**Sage brown butter**
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter
- 6 to 8 fresh sage leaves
- 1 tbsp maple syrup
- 1 tsp apple cider vinegar

**To finish**
- 2 tbsp toasted pepitas
- Flaky salt
- Crumbled feta or goat cheese (optional)

**Method**
1. **Heat:** Preheat the oven to 425°F with a rack in the lower third. Line a sheet pan with parchment.
2. **Cut:** Microwave each whole squash 2 minutes to soften it for cutting. Halve stem to base, scoop out the seeds, and cut into 1 inch wedges along the ridges. Leave the skin on.
3. **Season:** Toss the wedges with olive oil, salt, pepper, cinnamon and cayenne. Spread out cut side down with space between them.
4. **Roast:** Roast 15 minutes, flip, then roast 10 to 15 minutes more until tender and deeply browned at the edges.
5. **Brown butter:** While the squash roasts, melt butter in a small pan over medium. Add sage and cook 2 to 3 minutes until the butter smells nutty and turns golden brown and the sage is crisp. Off heat, stir in maple syrup and vinegar.
6. **Finish:** Spoon the brown butter and crispy sage over the hot squash. Top with pepitas, flaky salt and cheese if using.

**Notes**
- Halves instead of wedges: roast cut side down 30 to 35 minutes, then fill with the brown butter, or stuff with wild rice, sausage or quinoa.
- Delicata, carnival or acorn squash all work. Acorn skin is tougher, so add 5 to 10 minutes.
- Roast the seeds: rinse, dry, toss with oil and salt, and bake at 325°F for 15 to 20 minutes.
- Miso swap: whisk 1 tsp white miso into the brown butter instead of the vinegar.
- Good next to the jerk duck tacos, or on the Thanksgiving table.

## Split Pea Soup

A customizable split pea soup: smoky with bacon or ham hock, or vegetarian with smoked paprika. Adding the peas in two batches gives every spoonful some texture.

**Tags:** soup, split peas, make-ahead, vegetarian option, nyt cooking
**Time:** 2 hr 20 min

**Serves 4**

**Ingredients**
- 6 slices bacon, or 1 (1-pound) smoked ham hock, or 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, plus more as needed
- 2 medium leeks, white and pale green parts, rinsed and thinly sliced (about 1 3/4 cups)
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 medium onion, diced (about 1 1/2 cups)
- Salt and black pepper
- 2 medium carrots, scrubbed and diced (about 1 1/2 cups)
- 1 pound dried split green peas, divided
- 2 fresh thyme sprigs
- 8 to 12 cups chicken stock or water
- Lemon wedges, for serving (optional)

**Method**
1. **Start the base:** With bacon, cook it in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium until crisp, 12 to 13 minutes, then set it aside to crumble later and keep the fat in the pot. With a ham hock, heat the oil over medium-high and brown the hock until golden, about 6 minutes. For vegetarian, just heat the oil over medium.
2. **Vegetables:** Add the leeks, garlic and onion, season well with salt and pepper, and cook over medium, stirring, until tender, 5 to 8 minutes. Add a little oil if they look dry.
3. **First simmer:** Stir in the carrots, paprika (if using), half the split peas, the thyme and 8 cups stock or water. Bring to a boil, then simmer partly covered, stirring now and then, until the peas are tender, about 45 minutes.
4. **Mash:** Coarsely mash with a potato masher or a few pulses of an immersion blender, watching for the thyme sprigs. Blend longer for a smoother soup.
5. **Second simmer:** Add the remaining split peas and simmer until soft, about 1 hour. Add stock or water if it gets too thick, or simmer uncovered to thicken.
6. **Finish:** If using a ham hock, pull the meat off the bone, cube it and stir it in. Season with salt and pepper and serve with crumbled bacon and lemon wedges.

**Notes**
- By Ali Slagle for NYT Cooking.
- Make up to 1 week ahead. Add stock when reheating if it has thickened.

*Source: NYT Cooking (Ali Slagle): https://cooking.nytimes.com/search?q=Split%20Pea%20Soup*

## Butternut Squash Congee With Chile Oil

A seasonal congee made quickly from leftover rice. Butternut squash melts into a creamy, golden porridge, finished with scallions and chile oil.

**Tags:** congee, rice, vegetarian, leftovers, squash, fall, nyt cooking
**Time:** 45 min

**Serves 4 to 6**

**Ingredients**
- 2 tightly packed cups leftover medium- or long-grain rice, preferably refrigerated
- 1 tbsp neutral oil, such as canola
- 1 tsp kosher salt (such as Diamond Crystal), plus more to taste
- 6 cups vegetable stock
- 4 garlic cloves, peeled and crushed
- 1 pound butternut squash (about 1/2 small), peeled, seeded and cut into 1-inch pieces
- 1 small piece kombu (optional)
- 2 scallions, finely sliced
- Chile oil or chile crisp, for serving

**Method**
1. **Combine:** In a large pot, stir together the rice, oil and salt, breaking up any clumps. Add the stock, garlic, squash and kombu (if using) and bring to a boil over medium-high.
2. **Simmer:** Reduce the heat to low, cover and simmer 30 minutes.
3. **Break down:** Uncover, stir, raise the heat to medium and simmer 5 to 10 minutes more, until the rice has broken down.
4. **Stir:** Turn off the heat and remove any large pieces of kombu (leaving some is fine). Stir vigorously with a wooden spoon to break up the rice, squash and garlic. Some squash stays in pieces; the rest turns the congee golden. Salt to taste.
5. **Serve:** Top with scallions and a few drops of chile oil or chile crisp.

**Notes**
- By Hetty Lui McKinnon for NYT Cooking.
- The vigorous final stir is what makes it creamy.
- Easier squash prep: cut it into smaller pieces before peeling, or buy pre-cut cubes.

*Source: NYT Cooking (Hetty Lui McKinnon): https://cooking.nytimes.com/search?q=Butternut%20Squash%20Congee%20With%20Chile%20Oil*

## Red Peas Soup

Hearty Jamaican red kidney bean soup with smoked turkey, coconut milk and optional dumplings. It's thick and spicy enough to pass as a stew.

**Tags:** jamaican, soup, beans, smoked turkey, nyt cooking
**Time:** about 3 hr (plus overnight soaking)

**Serves 6 generously**

**Soup**
- 2 cups dried red kidney beans
- 1 pound smoked turkey necks
- 8 cups water or low-sodium chicken stock
- 1 (13.5-ounce) can coconut milk
- 1 (1.75-ounce) package cock-flavored soup mix, such as Grace
- 4 garlic cloves, chopped
- 4 scallions, halved crosswise
- 4 thyme sprigs
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 1 Scotch bonnet pepper, pierced (not sliced)
- 1 standard chicken bouillon cube
- 1 tsp ground allspice
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 1 medium sweet potato or yam, diced
- 1 carrot, diced
- Cooked white rice, for serving (optional)

**Dumplings** (optional)
- 2 cups self-rising flour
- 1 tsp kosher salt

**Method**
1. **Soak:** Cover the beans with 4 to 6 cups water in a large bowl and soak overnight.
2. **Turkey:** Put the smoked turkey in a large, heavy pot with enough water or stock to cover (about 8 cups). Boil over medium 30 minutes, skimming off the foam. Drain the beans and add them to the pot.
3. **Simmer:** Add the coconut milk, soup mix, garlic, scallions, thyme, onion, Scotch bonnet, bouillon, allspice and black pepper. Simmer over medium until the beans are tender, 1 to 2 hours, adding water as needed.
4. **Dumplings:** If using, mix the flour and salt, then stir in 1 cup water until shaggy and wet.
5. **Vegetables:** Lift out the turkey, pull the meat from the bones and return it to the pot. Add the sweet potato and carrot, drop in spoonfuls of dumpling dough, and cook over medium, flipping the dumplings once or twice, until everything is tender, about 15 minutes.
6. **Finish:** Discard the thyme, Scotch bonnet and scallions. Adjust the seasoning, thin with water if it's too thick, and serve hot, over rice if you like.

**Notes**
- By Millie Peartree for NYT Cooking.
- Smoked turkey necks stand in for the salted pig tails some versions use; either brings deep smokiness.
- Look for cock-flavored soup mix in the international aisle.

*Source: NYT Cooking (Millie Peartree): https://cooking.nytimes.com/search?q=Red%20Peas%20Soup*

## Soup Joumou

Haitian squash soup with epis-marinated beef, vegetables, vermicelli and dumplings. Haitians traditionally eat it on Jan. 1 to celebrate independence.

**Tags:** haitian, soup, squash, beef, holiday, nyt cooking
**Time:** 2 hr (plus marinating)

**Makes about 20 cups (6 to 10 servings)**

**Epis seasoning**
- 1 large green bell pepper, stemmed and chopped
- 1 medium red bell pepper, stemmed and chopped
- 1/2 white onion, chopped
- 1/4 cup white vinegar
- 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 celery stalk, chopped
- 2 scallions, trimmed and chopped
- 1 fresh parsley sprig
- 5 garlic cloves, peeled
- 1 chicken bouillon cube
- 1/2 tsp fresh or dried thyme leaves
- 1 1/2 tsp kosher salt

**Soup**
- 1 pound boneless beef chuck, cut into 1/2-inch pieces
- 1/2 cup fresh lime juice (from about 3 limes)
- 5 tbsp epis seasoning
- 2 pounds calabaza squash, peeled and roughly chopped
- 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 chicken bouillon cube
- 5 medium Idaho or russet potatoes, peeled and cut into 3/4-inch cubes
- 1/2 small head green cabbage, shredded (about 3 cups)
- 4 medium carrots, peeled and sliced
- 1 large white onion, finely chopped
- 2 celery stalks, cut into 1/2-inch pieces
- 2 scallions, trimmed and sliced
- 3 fresh parsley sprigs
- 1 Scotch bonnet pepper
- 1/4 tsp fresh or dried thyme leaves
- 2 tsp kosher salt
- 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 1/2 cup vermicelli

**Dumplings**
- 1 1/4 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1/4 tsp kosher salt
- 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper

**Method**
1. **Epis:** Purée all the epis ingredients in a blender. Use now or refrigerate in a jar up to 6 weeks.
2. **Marinate:** Rinse the beef in lukewarm water, then rub it all over with the lime juice. Toss with 5 tbsp epis, cover and refrigerate at least 30 minutes, ideally overnight.
3. **Squash:** Purée the calabaza with 1 cup water in a blender and set aside.
4. **Brown:** Heat the oil in a large pot over medium. Add the beef with its marinade, the tomato paste and the bouillon cube, crumbled. Cook, stirring occasionally, until it caramelizes and darkens, about 10 minutes. Move the meat to a bowl.
5. **Build the soup:** Add 6 cups water to the pot and bring to a boil over high. Stir in the squash purée, potatoes, cabbage, carrots, onion, celery, scallions, parsley, Scotch bonnet, thyme, salt and pepper and return to a boil. Stir in the beef and its juices.
6. **Simmer:** Once boiling, cover, reduce to low and simmer 30 minutes, stirring occasionally.
7. **Vermicelli:** Carefully remove the Scotch bonnet without letting it burst. Stir in the vermicelli and cook 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
8. **Dumplings:** Meanwhile, stir the flour, oil, salt, pepper and 1/2 cup water into a dough, kneading lightly if needed. Roll 1-tablespoon pieces between your palms into logs about 4 inches long and drop them into the soup as you go. Cook on low, stirring now and then, until cooked through but still firm, 15 to 20 minutes.
9. **Serve:** Thin with about 1 cup water if you like, then serve warm.

**Notes**
- From Cindy Similien's "Let's Speak Haitian Food," adapted by Priya Krishna for NYT Cooking.
- Leftover epis keeps 6 weeks and works like sofrito: marinate beef, fish or chicken, or stir it into rice and soups.
- If the Scotch bonnet bursts, the soup gets very spicy.

*Source: NYT Cooking (Cindy Similien, adapted by Priya Krishna): https://cooking.nytimes.com/search?q=Soup%20Joumou*

## Nonna's Sunday Sugo (2 Can Version)

**Tags:** italian, sauce, tomato, sunday, make-ahead, vegetarian
**Time:** 2 hr 30 min

**Makes enough for about 1.5 lb pasta**

**Ingredients**
- 2 (28 oz) cans San Marzano whole peeled tomatoes (DOP)
- 1/4 cup extra virgin olive oil
- 1 medium yellow onion, diced
- 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1/2 cup dry red wine
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- Pinch of red pepper flakes
- Small handful fresh basil, torn
- Salt to taste
- 1 Parmigiano Reggiano rind (optional, recommended)

**Method**
1. Hand crush tomatoes into a bowl.
2. Olive oil over medium-low. Cook onion with a pinch of salt 8 to 10 minutes until soft (don't brown). Add garlic and pepper flakes 1 minute.
3. Add tomato paste, caramelize 2 minutes.
4. Deglaze with red wine, reduce until mostly evaporated.
5. Add tomatoes plus 1/3 can of water, oregano, half the basil, Parm rind, salt.
6. Gentle simmer, then lowest heat, lid cracked. Cook 2 hours minimum, stirring every 20 minutes.
7. Remove rind, stir in remaining basil. Meatballs go in for the last 45 to 60 minutes.
8. Serve over rigatoni or spaghetti with Pecorino Romano.

**Making it a day early:** Cool uncovered 30 minutes, then refrigerate. Reheat low with a splash of water. Hold the final basil until reheating.

## Ponzu Sauce

**Tags:** japanese, sauce, citrus, make-ahead
**Time:** 10 min (plus 24 hr steep)

**Makes about 1 cup**

**Ingredients**
- 120ml soy sauce
- 120ml fresh citrus juice (yuzu, or half lemon half lime)
- 2 tbsp mirin
- 2 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 piece kombu (4 inch)
- 1/2 cup katsuobushi (bonito flakes), loosely packed

**Method**
1. Simmer mirin 30 seconds to cook off alcohol. Cool.
2. In a jar combine soy, vinegar, mirin, kombu, bonito. No citrus yet.
3. Refrigerate 24 hours (4 hours in a pinch).
4. Strain, pressing the bonito. Stir in citrus juice.
5. Rest 1 hour before using. Adjust to taste.

**Notes:** Keeps 2 to 3 months refrigerated. Great with shabu-shabu, gyoza, sashimi, salads, grilled fish. Same-day shortcut: whisk soy, citrus, mirin, vinegar, and a pinch of dashi powder.

## Kimchi

**Tags:** korean, fermented, project
**Time:** 1 hr active (plus 1 to 3 days fermenting)

**Ingredients**
- 1 head napa cabbage (about 2 lb)
- 1/2 cup coarse sea salt
- 1/2 cup gochugaru
- 3 tbsp fish sauce
- 1 tbsp salted shrimp paste
- 5 to 6 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
- 1 tsp sugar (or rice flour porridge: 1 tbsp rice flour cooked briefly in 1/2 cup water)
- Scallions, sliced
- Daikon, matchstick cut
- Carrot, julienned (optional)

**Method**
1. **Salt:** Cut cabbage into bite-size pieces. Toss with salt, rest 1 to 2 hours, tossing occasionally. Rinse 2 to 3 times, drain well.
2. **Paste:** Mix gochugaru, fish sauce, shrimp paste, garlic, ginger, sugar or porridge.
3. **Combine:** Wearing gloves, massage paste into cabbage, scallions, daikon, carrot.
4. **Pack:** Press tightly into a clean jar, submerged in its own liquid, 1 inch headspace. Seal loosely.
5. **Ferment:** Room temp 1 to 3 days, pressing down once or twice daily. Refrigerate once tangy enough.

**Notes:** Use real gochugaru; generic chili flakes won't give the right flavor or color. The rice flour porridge feeds the bacteria for a stronger ferment. Flavor keeps developing in the fridge for weeks.
